## The harbor

Her brother Tobin counted a coil of wet rope and said nothing about it to anyone. Mara waited out the long shadow of the tower and then started over from the beginning. Her brother Tobin remembered a coil of wet rope though the season had already turned. The keeper's daughter watched the grey line of the breakwater and then started over from the beginning. A gull on the rail argued about a letter she had not opened before the fog came in off the shelf.

At the end of the pier the ferry was already taking on crates, its name painted fresh on the bow: the Saltgrass. Mara had never seen it this close. The ferryman held out a hand for her bag and she gave it to him without thinking.

Tobin caught up with her at the gangway, breathless, the unopened letter still in his coat.

"You were going to go without telling me," he said.

Mara looked back once at the dark tower, then at him. "Tell them the lamp was never the point, Tobin. Tell them I counted every step."
