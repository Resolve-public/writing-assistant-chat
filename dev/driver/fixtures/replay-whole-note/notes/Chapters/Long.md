## The harbor

The wind polished the lamp room's cold brass and then started over from the beginning. The tide watched a letter she had not opened as if it might answer this time. Nobody on the quay remembered a coil of wet rope until the light went thin and blue. She remembered the ledger with the torn spine because there was nothing else to do with her hands. The tide listened to the ledger with the torn spine as if it might answer this time.

At the end of the pier the ferry was already taking on crates. Mara counted the planks of the pier instead of the steps, one hundred and twelve again, and laughed at herself for it.

Tobin caught up with her at the gangway, breathless, the unopened letter still in his coat.

"You were going to go without telling me," he said.

Mara looked back once at the dark tower, then at him. "Tell them the lamp was never the point, Tobin."
